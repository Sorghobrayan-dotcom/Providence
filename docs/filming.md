# The three minutes

Written to be held beside you while recording. Every number in it was measured
today against the deployed site, not estimated.

## What to record with

Nothing to buy and nothing to install. Windows 11 has both halves already:

- **Win + G** opens the Xbox Game Bar. In the *Capture* widget, turn the
  microphone on, press record. It records the window you are in.
- **Clipchamp** (in the Start menu) trims and joins clips, and exports 1080p.

Record in **two or three takes** rather than one. A fluffed sentence is then
twenty seconds to redo, not three minutes. Clipchamp joins them end to end.

Before you start: full-screen the browser on the deployed site, close the tour if
it offers itself, and open the **Soul** tab. Speak slowly. Silence while
something happens on screen is fine — it is better than filling it.

---

## The spine

Three beats, and they are in this order because each one only lands if the
previous one has.

1. **The gap** — what engines cannot do. 25 seconds.
2. **Peter** — the whole thesis in one character. 90 seconds.
3. **Where Scripture actually is** — the console, live. 40 seconds.

Anything left over goes on Jonah or the donkey. If you run short, cut those two
and not the console.

---

## Beat 1 — the gap (0:00 – 0:25)

*On screen:* the site, still. Do not touch anything yet.

> "Your game engine computes where a falling body lands to a precision no human
> can match. Ask it what it costs when one character betrays another, and there
> is nothing there — to an engine, a murder and a handshake are the same event.
> A state change.
>
> Providence is the layer that knows the difference. Not a game. A library you
> put underneath one."

*Then:* click slowly down the behaviour library on the left so the 24 names
scroll past.

> "Twenty-four behaviours, taken from the text."

---

## Beat 2 — Peter (0:25 – 1:55)

This is the one that has to work. Rehearse it once before recording.

*Click* **Le Serment Brise**.

> "Peter. He is standing there and he is not your companion — nothing has been
> asked of him yet, and a promise nobody asked for cannot be broken later."

*Drag* in the viewport until you are next to him, *press* **E**.

> "Walk up to him and speak."

*Wait* for the plate — it takes about four seconds, because the line is being
generated from his portrait, his state, and what you have done. When the menu
appears, *click* **« Demande-lui de te suivre. »**

> "He swears he will go with me to prison and to death. That is Luke 22, and it
> is in the console under his own words."

*Toggle* **under threat** in the toolbar. Now say nothing for about eight
seconds and let the fear meter climb on screen.

> "Now the threat. Watch the fear, not the figure."

*It breaks past 0.7, on its own.*

> "He denies he has ever met me. Not 'stops helping' — denies."

*Toggle* **under threat** off.

> "He weeps and withdraws, and now nothing I press does anything. No gold, no
> persuasion check, no reputation bar to top up. Three kindnesses bring him
> back."

*Press* **K** three times. He returns.

*Now the part nobody else has.* Open **Relations**, *click* **betray peter**.

> "And here is what a number cannot say. I sell him — with no threat anywhere,
> nothing frightening him at all."

*He walks away, into `offended`.*

> "He does not deny me. He leaves. And betraying a sworn ally weighs twenty-one
> where a stranger weighs three, because the cost comes from the bond that
> broke, not from a table. Four kindnesses a betrayal is what winning him back
> costs — a brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city, and the arc
> charges exactly that."

*Press* **K** four times. He comes back.

> "Nothing in his behaviour mentions betrayal. It reads the graph."

---

## Beat 3 — where Scripture actually is (1:55 – 2:35)

*Point at the console* along the bottom.

> "Every line here carries the passage it came from. And the verse under it
> arrived from the YouVersion Platform a second ago, in French, live —
> nothing in this engine stores a word of Scripture. Pull the key and every
> character goes silent. There is a test that asserts exactly that, because a
> silence is honest and an invented line is not."

*Open* **Grace**.

> "And one thing the studio is forbidden to touch. Grace fires on one draw in
> five, and no game code can call it, no parameter shapes it, and its rate moves
> with nothing the player did. As far as we know no engine has ever shipped a
> feature its own authors are not allowed to tune. That absence is the feature."

---

## Close (2:35 – 3:00)

*Back to the viewport, still.*

> "Five hundred and twenty-two tests. A Godot addon that runs. A notebook that
> executes every claim in front of you.
>
> The brief asks for Scripture where people already are. Hundreds of millions
> live in game worlds. We did not build them a Bible. We put Scripture into the
> ground they walk on."

---

## If something goes wrong mid-take

- **He does not answer when you press E** — you are too far. Drag closer than
  three metres and try again.
- **The menu closes by itself** — his arc moved while it was open, which is
  deliberate: the soul does not wait for you. Say so and carry on; it is a good
  moment, not a broken one.
- **A character says nothing** — Gloo did not answer in time. He falls back to
  the passage rather than inventing a line. Also worth saying out loud.

## Last thing

Do not re-record for small stumbles. A voice that sounds like a person who built
something beats a voice that sounds like an advertisement, and the judges are
looking at the thing on screen.
